Each figure is the year’s import value divided by the quantity imported, both as recorded by the Department of Customs. A line covers every brand, grade and origin that falls under it, so the average sits between the cheapest and dearest goods in that line.

What this means if you trade

  • Use the average to sense-check a supplier’s quote. A quote far below it deserves a second look before customs valuation does the same.
  • Tax per piece is what was actually collected, after any exemption or SAFTA rate. The calculator gives the figure for your own invoice.
  • Every product page now shows this average for its own line.

How this was worked out

Every figure is calculated from the Department of Customs, Nepal, Foreign Trade Statistics, FY 2082/83, and the Nepal Customs Tariff 2083/84. Values are in Nepali rupees (1 crore = 10 million, 1 arba = 1 billion, 1 kharba = 100 billion). Average declared value = import value ÷ quantity for the 8-digit line. Lines with very small quantities are left out. The article reports what the data shows and does not explain causes it cannot show.
