Shipping & ocean freight to Nepal

Nepal is landlocked — it has no seaport. Every container of sea freight reaches Nepal the same way: by ocean to a gateway port in India or Bangladesh, then by rail or road to a Nepal dry port, where it is cleared by Nepal Customs. Here are the ports, the corridors, the transit times, and how to track it.

Nepal’s sea access runs on the Nepal–India Treaty of Transit (the main route, via Indian ports) and a Nepal–Bangladesh transit arrangement (an eastern alternative). Goods for China move overland instead — by road via Rasuwagadhi (Kerung) and Tatopani (Khasa) — not by sea.

The corridors

Eastern India corridor — the primary route

Ocean vessel → Kolkata / Haldia / Vizag → Broad-gauge rail → Raxaul (border) → Birgunj ICD → Nepal market

Western India corridor — for Europe, Gulf & the Americas

Ocean vessel → Nhava Sheva / Mundra → Rail (CONCOR) / road → Birgunj corridor → Birgunj / Bhairahawa ICD → Nepal market

Bangladesh corridor — an eastern alternative

Ocean vessel → Mongla / Chattogram → Banglabandha–Phulbari transit → Kakarbhitta (border) → Eastern Nepal

Gateway ports

The sea ports Nepal’s cargo passes through — in India and Bangladesh:

KolkataIndia · INCCU — Nepal's traditional sea gateway on the east coast.HaldiaIndia · INHAL — Deeper-draft dock complex downstream of Kolkata.VisakhapatnamIndia · INVTZ — Deep-water east-coast port, Nepal's fast-growing alternative to Kolkata.Nhava ShevaIndia · INNSA — India's largest container port — the west-coast gateway for Europe, Gulf & Americas cargo.MundraIndia · INMUN — India's largest private port (Gujarat) — a west-coast option with strong rail links.MonglaBangladesh · BDMGL — Bangladesh's second seaport — an eastern-corridor alternative for Nepal.ChattogramBangladesh · BDCGP — Bangladesh's principal seaport — available to Nepal under transit arrangements.
