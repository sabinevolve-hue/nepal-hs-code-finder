Nepal Customs Tariff 2083/84 (2026/27): what changed

The Nepal Customs Tariff 2083/84 (2026/27) is the Department of Customs’ official schedule of import duty for every HS code, read with the Customs Tariff Act and the Finance Act 2083. It applies to fiscal year 2083/84, which began on 1 Shrawan 2083 (17 July 2026)[5]. Download the official PDF from the Department of Customs tariff page.

What changed vs 2082/83

The changes below come from the FY 2083/84 budget, presented by Finance Minister Dr Swarnim Wagle on 15 Jestha 2083 (29 May 2026)[3], and the Finance Bill 2083 that carries them into law.

  1. Customs duty tiers cut from 11 to 7. The budget limited the existing eleven-tier customs rate structure to seven tiers.[2][3]
  2. Lower duty on industrial raw materials. Customs duty was reduced on 273 types of industrial raw materials, under a rule that raw-material duty is at least one tier below the duty on the finished goods made from them.[2][3]
  3. Customs-point levies merged into a green tax. Scattered charges collected at customs — the infrastructure development tax and the road maintenance and improvement fee — were folded into a single green tax.[2][3]
  4. Excise abolished on 360 goods. Not a customs-duty change, but it lowers the landed cost of those imports.[2][3]
  5. Fuel: 50% customs-duty concession. The Finance Bill 2083 gives a 50% reduction in customs duty on petrol (2710.12.10), kerosene (2710.19.10) and high-speed diesel (2710.19.30) from 15 Jestha 2083, a cut the government first made on 7 April 2026.[6][7][8] The government can change or withdraw it by notice in the Nepal Gazette.[7] See the note below on how this shows in the tariff book.
  6. Relief for property damaged in the Gen Z movement. Businesses importing goods to restore property damaged by arson or vandalism get a 50% discount on customs and excise duty, backed by an insurance surveyor’s report.[6][7]
Fuel rates: tariff book vs concession. The 2026/27 tariff book prints the full schedule rates: Motor spirit (petrol) (2710.12.10) Rs 25,200 per KL; Kerosene (superior kerosene oil) (2710.19.10) Rs 12,000 per KL; High-speed diesel (2710.19.30) Rs 12,000 per KL. The Finance Bill 2083 concession halves these: Infinity Law’s summary gives Rs 12,600 per KL for petrol and Rs 6,000 per KL for kerosene and high-speed diesel.[6] Because the government can change the concession by gazette notice, confirm the rate in force on your import date with the customs office.

Why there is no line-by-line comparison here

A true line-by-line diff needs both years’ tariff schedules in machine-readable form. Customs Nepal holds the full 2026/27 schedule but not the 2082/83 one, and the budget documents do not list the 273 raw materials item by item. Rather than guess, this page states only changes we can cite. To see the rate in force for your product, look up its HS code (below) — the site always shows the current 2026/27 rate.

The 2026/27 tariff in numbers

Computed from the official Customs Tariff 2026/27 (HS 2022, seventh edition) as transcribed on this site: 6,340 tariff lines across 96 chapters. The general duty column uses exactly seven ad-valorem rates — matching the seven-tier structure announced in the budget — plus 110 lines with specific per-unit or “whichever is higher” rates (mainly beverages and alcohol, fruit juices, tobacco and fuels).

Frequently asked questions

Where can I download the Nepal customs tariff 2083/84 PDF?

The official Customs Tariff 2026/27 (FY 2083/84) PDF is published by the Department of Customs on its Customs Tariff page at customs.gov.np/content/50/customs-tarrif/ (link: “Customs Tarrif 2026-27”). Customs Nepal does not re-host the PDF; every rate on this site is transcribed from it, and you can search it by product or HS code.

When did the 2083/84 customs tariff take effect?

The budget was presented on 15 Jestha 2083 (29 May 2026) and the Finance Bill 2083 passed the House of Representatives on 14 Asar 2083 (28 June 2026). Fiscal year 2083/84 began on 1 Shrawan 2083 (17 July 2026) with the Finance Act 2083 in force. Some Finance Bill measures, such as the 50% fuel customs-duty concession, applied from budget day. The official tariff book itself does not print a start date.

How many customs duty rates (tiers) are there in 2083/84?

The FY 2083/84 budget cut the customs duty structure from 11 tiers to 7. In the published 2026/27 tariff the general (ad-valorem) duty column uses seven rates: Free, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 30% and 80%. A further 110 tariff lines carry specific per-unit or “whichever is higher” rates, mainly beverages and alcohol, fruit juices, tobacco and fuels.

Which goods got lower customs duty in the 2083/84 budget?

The budget speech says customs duty was cut on 273 types of industrial raw materials, with a rule that raw-material duty stays at least one tier below the duty on finished goods. The speech and news coverage do not publish a line-by-line list, so check the current rate for your specific HS code in the search or calculator.

What is the bhansar (customs duty) rate on my product in 2026?

Search the product or its 8-digit HS code on customsnepal.com: each code shows the 2026/27 general and SAARC customs duty from the official tariff. The landed-cost calculator then adds 13% VAT (charged on value plus duty) and gives a rupee estimate.

Is the 2083/84 tariff the same as the 2026/27 tariff?

Yes. 2083/84 is Nepal's fiscal year in Bikram Sambat (mid-July 2026 to mid-July 2027); the Department of Customs prints the same book as “Customs Tariff 2026/2027”. It follows the Harmonized System 2022 (seventh edition).
