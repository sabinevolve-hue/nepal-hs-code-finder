Can I import this into Nepal?

Before the duty, there are the rules. Every commercial import needs an EXIM code, a compliant payment route and a customs declaration — and many products also need a permit from a specific authority. Paste an HS code to see which authority is likely involved, or read the full breakdown below.

Always required (any commercial import)

An importer registrationA registered firm with a PAN/VAT number and an EXIM (export-import) code from the Department of Customs.
A compliant payment routeMost commercial imports need a letter of credit (L/C) or another Nepal Rastra Bank–permitted payment method; some require advance-payment or document-against-payment approval.
A customs declarationA declaration through ASYCUDA at the customs office / dry port, with the invoice, packing list, bill of lading / airway bill and certificate of origin, and payment of duty + 13% VAT (and any excise).

Product-specific permits — who governs what

On top of the universal requirements, these authorities control imports of particular products. The checker above maps your HS code to the ones likely to apply.

Banned & restricted goods

A few goods are prohibited or quantity-restricted under the Export Import (Control) Act and periodic government notices (the list changes — past examples have included certain luxury items during balance-of-payments measures). Narcotics, counterfeit goods and unlicensed arms are always prohibited. Check the current control list on the Department of Customs and Nepal Trade Information Portal before committing.
