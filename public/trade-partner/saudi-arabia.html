7 years of trade

Since FY 2076/77, Nepal’s imports from Saudi Arabia moved from Rs 7.44 arba to Rs 9.27 arba (+25%) and exports from Rs 1.2 crore to Rs 24.0 crore (+1912%).

FY 2083/84 so far (Shrawan–Asoj (first 3 months)): imports Rs 11.56 arba · exports Rs 24 lakh. Provisional, from the latest monthly release; it will be replaced by the full-year figure.

What Nepal imports from Saudi Arabia

No SAFTA / preferential rate. Saudi Arabia is not a SAARC member, so its goods pay Nepal’s full general (MFN) duty — the rates shown below apply in full.

“Collected at customs” is the revenue the Department of Customs reports against each product from Saudi Arabia — customs duty plus the other taxes charged at the border (such as VAT and excise) — so it can be well above the duty rate alone, and is not zero just because the duty rate is.

What Nepal collects at the border

Nepal collected Rs 1.21 arba at customs on goods from Saudi Arabia — 0.2% of everything customs collected on imports that year. That is about Rs 13.1 for every Rs 100 of goods, below the Rs 24.1 average across all imports. The mix explains it: fuel, vehicles and consumer goods are taxed heavily; raw materials, machinery and many electronics lightly.

How goods move between Nepal and Saudi Arabia

By sea to an Indian gateway port, then overland. As a landlocked country Nepal has no sea port, so goods from Saudi Arabia arrive by ocean freight at an Indian gateway — Kolkata/Haldia or Visakhapatnam — then move by rail/road to the Birgunj (Sirsiya) ICD for customs clearance.

The opportunity, honestly

This is a sourcing relationship, not an export one — Nepal buys far more than it sells. The practical moves: check whether a SAARC source carries a lower SAFTA rate, compare the route and landed cost before committing, and watch budget-year duty changes on electronics and vehicles. Import demand is not the same as profit — treat these figures as a starting point for research.
