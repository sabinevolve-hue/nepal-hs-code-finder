7 years of trade

Since FY 2076/77, Nepal’s imports from Uruguay moved from Rs 4.5 crore to Rs 45.9 crore (+909%) and exports from Rs 24 lakh to Rs 94 lakh (+298%).

What Nepal imports from Uruguay

No SAFTA / preferential rate. Uruguay is not a SAARC member, so its goods pay Nepal’s full general (MFN) duty — the rates shown below apply in full.

“Collected at customs” is the revenue the Department of Customs reports against each product from Uruguay — customs duty plus the other taxes charged at the border (such as VAT and excise) — so it can be well above the duty rate alone, and is not zero just because the duty rate is.

What Nepal collects at the border

Nepal collected Rs 3 lakh at customs on goods from Uruguay — 0.0% of everything customs collected on imports that year. That is about Rs 0.1 for every Rs 100 of goods, below the Rs 24.1 average across all imports. The mix explains it: fuel, vehicles and consumer goods are taxed heavily; raw materials, machinery and many electronics lightly.

How goods move between Nepal and Uruguay

By sea to an Indian gateway port, then overland. As a landlocked country Nepal has no sea port, so goods from Uruguay arrive by ocean freight at an Indian gateway — Kolkata/Haldia or Visakhapatnam — then move by rail/road to the Birgunj (Sirsiya) ICD for customs clearance.

The opportunity, honestly

This is a sourcing relationship, not an export one — Nepal buys far more than it sells. The practical moves: check whether a SAARC source carries a lower SAFTA rate, compare the route and landed cost before committing, and watch budget-year duty changes on electronics and vehicles. Import demand is not the same as profit — treat these figures as a starting point for research.
