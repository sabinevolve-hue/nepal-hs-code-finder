What Nepal imports from China

No SAFTA / preferential rate. China is not a SAARC member, so its goods pay Nepal’s full general (MFN) duty — the rates shown below apply in full.

“Collected at customs” is the revenue the Department of Customs reports against each product from China — customs duty plus the other taxes charged at the border (such as VAT and excise) — so it can be well above the duty rate alone, and is not zero just because the duty rate is.

What Nepal collects at the border

Nepal collected Rs 94.13 arba at customs on goods from China — 19.7% of everything customs collected on imports that year. That is about Rs 27.6 for every Rs 100 of goods, close to the Rs 26.4 average across all imports. The mix explains it: fuel, vehicles and consumer goods are taxed heavily; raw materials, machinery and many electronics lightly.

How goods move between Nepal and China

Overland, over the Himalaya — not by sea. China trade runs by road across the mountains: Rasuwagadhi–Kerung (Gyirong), Nepal's main China gate, and the historic Tatopani–Khasa (Zhangmu) crossing. A Kerung–Kathmandu rail link is proposed, not built.

The opportunity, honestly

This is a sourcing relationship, not an export one — Nepal buys far more than it sells. The practical moves: check whether a SAARC source carries a lower SAFTA rate, compare the route and landed cost before committing, and watch budget-year duty changes on electronics and vehicles. Import demand is not the same as profit — treat these figures as a starting point for research.
