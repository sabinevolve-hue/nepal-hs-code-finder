“Collected at customs” is the revenue the Department of Customs reports against each product from Ireland — customs duty plus the other taxes charged at the border (such as VAT and excise) — so it can be well above the duty rate alone, and is not zero just because the duty rate is.

What Nepal collects at the border

Nepal collected Rs 30.6 crore at customs on goods from Ireland — 0.1% of everything customs collected on imports that year. That is about Rs 30.1 for every Rs 100 of goods, above the Rs 24.1 average across all imports. The mix explains it: fuel, vehicles and consumer goods are taxed heavily; raw materials, machinery and many electronics lightly.

How goods move between Nepal and Ireland

By sea to an Indian gateway port, then overland. As a landlocked country Nepal has no sea port, so goods from Ireland arrive by ocean freight at an Indian gateway — Kolkata/Haldia or Visakhapatnam — then move by rail/road to the Birgunj (Sirsiya) ICD for customs clearance.

The opportunity, honestly

Nepal has a real two-way relationship here. On the export side, look at where Ireland already buys heavily and where Nepal has genuine product (herbs, carpets, felt, pashmina, specialty agro). Import demand is not the same as profit — treat these figures as a starting point for research.
