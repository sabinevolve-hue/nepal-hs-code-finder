4 years of trade

Since FY 2079/80, Nepal’s imports from Czechia moved from Rs 71.9 crore to Rs 54.2 crore (−25%) and exports from Rs 22.0 crore to Rs 19.9 crore (−9%).

FY 2083/84 so far (Shrawan–Bhadra (first 2 months)): imports Rs 8.2 crore · exports Rs 5.1 crore. Provisional, from the latest monthly release; it will be replaced by the full-year figure.

“Collected at customs” is the revenue the Department of Customs reports against each product from Czechia — customs duty plus the other taxes charged at the border (such as VAT and excise) — so it can be well above the duty rate alone, and is not zero just because the duty rate is.

What Nepal collects at the border

Nepal collected Rs 10.1 crore at customs on goods from Czechia — 0.0% of everything customs collected on imports that year. That is about Rs 18.7 for every Rs 100 of goods, below the Rs 24.1 average across all imports. The mix explains it: fuel, vehicles and consumer goods are taxed heavily; raw materials, machinery and many electronics lightly.

How goods move between Nepal and Czechia

By sea to an Indian gateway port, then overland. As a landlocked country Nepal has no sea port, so goods from Czechia arrive by ocean freight at an Indian gateway — Kolkata/Haldia or Visakhapatnam — then move by rail/road to the Birgunj (Sirsiya) ICD for customs clearance.

Nepal has a real two-way relationship here. On the export side, look at where Czechia already buys heavily and where Nepal has genuine product (herbs, carpets, felt, pashmina, specialty agro). Import demand is not the same as profit — treat these figures as a starting point for research.
