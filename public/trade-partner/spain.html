4 years of trade

Since FY 2079/80, Nepal’s imports from Spain moved from Rs 1.42 arba to Rs 1.66 arba (+17%) and exports from Rs 38.9 crore to Rs 37.1 crore (−5%).

“Collected at customs” is the revenue the Department of Customs reports against each product from Spain — customs duty plus the other taxes charged at the border (such as VAT and excise) — so it can be well above the duty rate alone, and is not zero just because the duty rate is.

What Nepal collects at the border

Nepal collected Rs 33.4 crore at customs on goods from Spain — 0.1% of everything customs collected on imports that year. That is about Rs 20.1 for every Rs 100 of goods, below the Rs 24.1 average across all imports. The mix explains it: fuel, vehicles and consumer goods are taxed heavily; raw materials, machinery and many electronics lightly.

How goods move between Nepal and Spain

By sea to an Indian gateway port, then overland. As a landlocked country Nepal has no sea port, so goods from Spain arrive by ocean freight at an Indian gateway — Kolkata/Haldia or Visakhapatnam — then move by rail/road to the Birgunj (Sirsiya) ICD for customs clearance.
