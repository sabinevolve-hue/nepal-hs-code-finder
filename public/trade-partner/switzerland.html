What Nepal imports from Switzerland

No SAFTA / preferential rate. Switzerland is not a SAARC member, so its goods pay Nepal’s full general (MFN) duty — the rates shown below apply in full.

“Collected at customs” is the revenue the Department of Customs reports against each product from Switzerland — customs duty plus the other taxes charged at the border (such as VAT and excise) — so it can be well above the duty rate alone, and is not zero just because the duty rate is.

What Nepal collects at the border

Nepal collected Rs 37.8 crore at customs on goods from Switzerland — 0.1% of everything customs collected on imports that year. That is about Rs 25.7 for every Rs 100 of goods, close to the Rs 26.4 average across all imports. The mix explains it: fuel, vehicles and consumer goods are taxed heavily; raw materials, machinery and many electronics lightly.

How goods move between Nepal and Switzerland

By sea to an Indian gateway port, then overland. As a landlocked country Nepal has no sea port, so goods from Switzerland arrive by ocean freight at an Indian gateway — Kolkata/Haldia or Visakhapatnam — then move by rail/road to the Birgunj (Sirsiya) ICD for customs clearance.
