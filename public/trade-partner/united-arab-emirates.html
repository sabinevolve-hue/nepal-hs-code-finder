4 years of trade

Since FY 2079/80, Nepal’s imports from United Arab Emirates moved from Rs 32.21 arba to Rs 52.17 arba (+62%) and exports from Rs 1.08 arba to Rs 1.84 arba (+71%).

FY 2083/84 so far (Shrawan–Bhadra (first 2 months)): imports Rs 2.51 arba · exports Rs 41.2 crore. Provisional, from the latest monthly release; it will be replaced by the full-year figure.

What Nepal imports from United Arab Emirates

No SAFTA / preferential rate. United Arab Emirates is not a SAARC member, so its goods pay Nepal’s full general (MFN) duty — the rates shown below apply in full.

“Collected at customs” is the revenue the Department of Customs reports against each product from United Arab Emirates — customs duty plus the other taxes charged at the border (such as VAT and excise) — so it can be well above the duty rate alone, and is not zero just because the duty rate is.

What Nepal collects at the border

Nepal collected Rs 5.97 arba at customs on goods from United Arab Emirates — 1.2% of everything customs collected on imports that year. That is about Rs 11.5 for every Rs 100 of goods, below the Rs 24.1 average across all imports. The mix explains it: fuel, vehicles and consumer goods are taxed heavily; raw materials, machinery and many electronics lightly.

How goods move between Nepal and United Arab Emirates

By sea to an Indian gateway port, then overland. As a landlocked country Nepal has no sea port, so goods from United Arab Emirates arrive by ocean freight at an Indian gateway — Kolkata/Haldia or Visakhapatnam — then move by rail/road to the Birgunj (Sirsiya) ICD for customs clearance.

The opportunity, honestly

This is a sourcing relationship, not an export one — Nepal buys far more than it sells. The practical moves: check whether a SAARC source carries a lower SAFTA rate, compare the route and landed cost before committing, and watch budget-year duty changes on electronics and vehicles. Import demand is not the same as profit — treat these figures as a starting point for research.
