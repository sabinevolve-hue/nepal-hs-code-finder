Trade under sanctions

7 economies that are named in sanctions programmes and do not report complete trade figures. Their trade is read here from what their partners record.

When a country does not report its trade, that trade does not disappear from the record: every shipment is also an import or export in someone else's books. These pages follow each economy's recorded exports and imports by partner from 2010 to 2025, year by year and month by month, and say plainly what that method misses.

How to read these figures. Each of these economies does not report complete trade figures, so every number here, for every year, is what its trading partners record: their imports from it and their exports to it, as collected by the IMF. That keeps the years comparable, and it leaves gaps. The first two mean the recorded totals understate the real trade.
  • Partners that do not report either. Trade between two economies that both withhold data is missing, or is an IMF estimate.
  • Goods declared under another origin. Cargo that is relabelled or transshipped is recorded against the other country, not this one.
  • Valuation. Exports here are partners' import records, which include freight and insurance, so they run a little above the figures on the country profile.
This page reports the recorded figures only. It does not estimate unrecorded trade.

Which economies are here

An economy is included when it is named in a country sanctions programme of the United States Treasury on the retrieval date and does not report complete trade figures to the IMF. It is a statement about the data, not a judgement on any programme. Many other governments and the United Nations run programmes of their own.
