About the product

Large cardamom (Amomum subulatum), known locally as alaichi, is grown in Nepal's eastern hill districts — Taplejung, Panchthar, Ilam and Sankhuwasabha. Nepal is the world's top producer, and cardamom is one of its highest-value agricultural exports.

Nepal's position

Almost all of Nepal's crop goes to India, which both consumes it and re-exports onward to the Middle East and beyond. Because the market is so India-concentrated, prices swing with Indian demand and with how the India–Nepal trade treaty treats the product in a given year — a key risk for growers and exporters.

How to export from Nepal

  1. Register for an EXIM code with the Department of Customs (one-time exporter registration).
  2. Phytosanitary certificate from the National Plant Quarantine Program — required for agricultural produce.
  3. Certificate of origin from a recognised chamber of commerce or TEPC (a SAARC/SAFTA certificate for shipments to India).
  4. Grading & quality to the buyer's specification (moisture, size, cleanliness).
  5. File the export declaration in the customs system and clear at the border/dry port.

Agro exports may qualify for the government's cash export-incentive scheme; rates are set in the annual budget, so confirm the current figure with TEPC before you price a shipment.
