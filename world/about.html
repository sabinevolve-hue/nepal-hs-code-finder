About World Trade Data

What this site is for, where every figure comes from, and how far to trust it.

The goal

Trade statistics are public, but they are scattered across agencies, published in different currencies and formats, and hard to read. World Trade Data puts them in one place and shows, for each of 207 economies, what it sells, what it buys and with whom, in plain words and charts. It is free, needs no sign-in and carries no advertising.

The site does not produce statistics of its own. Every number is taken from a published official source, named on the page where it appears, with the year it refers to.

How to read the numbers

  • Two kinds of figure. The detailed 2024 pages use reconciled figures (BACI), where each export is matched to the partner's import record. The latest-year sections use customs figures as each economy reports them. The two can differ for the same country and year, sometimes by a lot, so each section says which it uses. Do not add or compare across them.
  • Currency. Everything is in current US dollars. Where the source publishes in another currency, each month is converted at that month's average exchange rate and the months are added up. Figures are not adjusted for inflation.
  • Exports and imports are valued differently. In customs figures, imports usually include freight and insurance and exports do not, so two countries' figures for the same flow rarely match.
  • Derived values. When an economy has not yet reported a recent flow, the IMF derives it from the partner's records. These are marked † and should be treated as provisional.
  • Goods only. Services, such as tourism, transport and software, are not included.
  • Small flows and small economies. Very small flows can be missing, and a few territories have no map shape or no profile.

How often it is updated

The latest-year figures are refreshed monthly. The detailed partner and product year moves forward once a year, when CEPII publishes a new BACI release, usually in January. Latest-year figures on this build were retrieved on 2026-11-15. Earlier years are kept on the site as new ones are added.

Using the figures

You may quote and share anything here. Please name the original source shown on the page, for example “Source: BACI (CEPII), via World Trade Data”. Each country profile has a PDF trade brief you can download and pass on.

Nothing here is advice. For a business or legal decision, check the figure with the source agency.
